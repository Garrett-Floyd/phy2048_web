Theorem 3.2.2.

\begin{equation*} v_{x,f} = \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}a_{x}dt + v_{x,i} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Recall from equation (3.1.2) that
\begin{equation*} a_{x} = \frac{dv_{x}}{dt}. \end{equation*}
Integrating both sides with respect to time yields
\begin{equation*} \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} a_{x} dt = \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} \frac{dv_{x}}{dt} dt. \end{equation*}
We now use the chain rule on the right hand side of the equation to transform it from an integral with respect to \(t\) to an integral with respect to \(v_{x}\text{.}\) This gives
\begin{align*} \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} a_{x} dt \amp = \int_{v_{x,i}}^{v_{x,f}} dv_{x} \\ \\ \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} a_{x} dt \amp = v_{x} \Bigg|^{v_{x,f}}_{v_{x,i}} \\ \\ \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} a_{x} dt \amp = v_{x,f} - v_{x,i} \\ \\ \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} a_{x} dt + v_{x,i} \amp = v_{x,f} \\ \\ v_{x,f} \amp = \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} a_{x} dt + v_{x,i}. \end{align*}
