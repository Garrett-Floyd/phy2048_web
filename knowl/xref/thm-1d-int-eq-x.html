Theorem 3.2.1.

\begin{equation*} x_{f} = \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}v_{x}dt + x_{i} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Recall from equation (3.1.1) that
\begin{equation*} v_{x} = \frac{dx}{dt}. \end{equation*}
Integrating both sides with respect to time yields
\begin{equation*} \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} v_{x} dt = \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} \frac{dx}{dt} dt. \end{equation*}
We now use the chain rule on the right hand side of the equation to transform it from an integral with respect to \(t\) to an integral with respect to \(x\text{.}\) This gives
\begin{align*} \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} v_{x} dt \amp = \int_{x_{i}}^{x_{f}} dx \\ \\ \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} v_{x} dt \amp = x \Bigg|^{x_{f}}_{x_{i}} \\ \\ \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} v_{x} dt \amp = x_{f} - x_{i} \\ \\ \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} v_{x} dt + x_{i} \amp = x_{f} \\ \\ x_{f} \amp = \int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} v_{x} dt + x_{i}. \end{align*}
